Dr. Vivek Rudrapatna's Clinical Data Science Group, UCSF · San Francisco, CA

  • Opened a second workstream in the same collaboration and reframed trial matching as a point-in-time decision — “from only the notes written before hour t, is this patient screenable now?” — rather than retrospective derivation from discharge summaries, which are unavailable at the moment a screening decision is actually made.
  • Designed a criterion-conditioned attention model over timestamped note streams with per-head learned recency decay: a frozen encoder embeds and caches each note chunk once, so only a 1.8 × 105-parameter head trains on CPU with no GPU and no LLM API at inference. Encoding is paid once per patient rather than once per patient×trial, so adding the 50th trial costs one criterion encode instead of another full pass over the census.
  • Beat both open-weight LLM generations at ∼107 times less inference compute: AUROC 0.753 [0.687, 0.810] versus 0.634 (Qwen2.5-7B) and 0.643 (Qwen3-8B) zero-shot over 363 paired patient–criterion decisions across 312 MIMIC-III patients, paired bootstrap deltas +0.119 and +0.109 with confidence intervals excluding zero; also clears an XGBoost-on-structured-labs baseline at 0.722.
  • Built the evaluation to attack its own result: identified that four of five eligibility criteria are defined by the very lab value they would be predicted from, so a naive run scores AUROC 1.000 and measures nothing, then reported results only on the criterion requiring clinical judgment and added leave-one-concept-out and note-volume-only null arms to catch accuracy that comes from counting notes rather than reading them.
  • Extended the same cached representations to a second task graded by an answer key the model never sees — whether a criterion is ascertainable at t, labeled exactly and at zero annotation cost from lab result timestamps (0.601 ± 0.008 over 2,505 decisions versus 0.504 for the volume-only control) — and showed that 14.5% of patient–criterion pairs change ascertainability status between hour 6 and hour 24, most of it evidence expiring rather than resolving. Split-conformal thresholding gives a distribution-free recall guarantee; manuscript in preparation with UCSF corpus validation planned.
